Property A
Stay 01

Ten Thousand Waves

Houses of the Moon — a Japanese onsen transplanted to the high desert

Inspired by Japan's mountain hot spring resorts, refined for forty-five years on twenty acres of piñon forest just outside town. Seventeen ryokan-style suites, nine hot baths, an izakaya restaurant. Featured in 1,000 Places to Visit Before You Die.

The Spa — Japanese Onsen Tradition

Private hot tub suites and the Grand Bath

The Grand Bath is modeled on Japanese onsen — a sloping shallow basin you sit in, choosing your depth, beneath a cascade waterfall. Cold plunge, sauna, meditation room. Massage and bodywork in serene treatment rooms.

Rooms

Cottages with natural wood, traditional artwork, modern luxuries. Two-night minimum.

Dining

Izanami — traditional Japanese izakaya, local ingredients, floor seating option.

Property D
Stay 04

Bishop's Lodge,
Auberge Resorts

317 acres in the Sangre de Cristo foothills — Archbishop Lamy's 19th-century retreat, reimagined

Originally Archbishop Jean-Baptiste Lamy's private mountain refuge in the 1860s. Now an Auberge Resorts Collection property — a complete reimagining opened 2021 across 100+ guest rooms, casitas, and residences. Ten minutes from the Plaza, set into the canyon at Tesuque with riding stables, hiking trails, and the original Chapel of San José still on the grounds.

SHEY Spa — Indigenous Wellness

Treatments rooted in the land and Pueblo tradition

SHEY (Tewa for "spirit") draws on Native New Mexican healing — turquoise rituals, sage and piñon body treatments, hot stone using river rocks from the property. Outdoor relaxation courtyards, sauna, steam, and a heated mineral pool overlooking the canyon.

Rooms

Casitas and suites with kiva fireplaces, private patios, locally-woven textiles, deep soaking tubs.

Dining

SkyFire — modern Southwestern by Chef Juan Bochenski. Bar Lamy for cocktails. Pool restaurant in summer.

A day in Santa Fe

Art — Morning
Canyon Road

Over 100 galleries packed into a half-mile of historic adobe — Native American antiques, contemporary, Western. Park before 10am for free street parking. Most galleries open daily 10–5.

Art — Mid-morning
Georgia O'Keeffe Museum

The single largest collection of O'Keeffe's work. Two blocks from the Plaza. Plan an hour and book ahead in summer.

History
Palace of the Governors

Built in 1610 — the oldest continuously occupied public building in the U.S. The Native American Vendors Program runs daily under the portal.

French / Architecture
Cathedral Basilica of St. Francis

French Romanesque cathedral built 1869–1887 by Archbishop Lamy, who preferred things French. A deliberate departure from the local adobe tradition.

French / Chapel
Loretto Chapel

Gothic Revival chapel by French architects Antoine and Projectus Mouly. The famous unsupported spiral staircase — its origin is the stuff of legend.

Antiques
Morning Star Gallery

Premier dealer of antique Native American art on Canyon Road — Southwest and Plains pieces.

Books
Collected Works Bookstore

Independent bookseller and coffeehouse near the Plaza since 1978.

Market — Saturday only
Santa Fe Farmers Market

Saturday mornings in the Railyard. Strict standards — local growers, makers, baked goods.

A day in Park City

Art — Main Street
Park City Gallery Association

Sixteen galleries along Historic Main Street — wildlife photography, contemporary, Indigenous-rooted. A walkable scene.

Art — Institution
Kimball Art Center

Free admission, rotating exhibitions of nationally recognized artists. The cultural anchor of Main Street.

History
Park City Museum

The mining town's silver-and-skiing story, set in the historic 1885 City Hall. Compact, well-curated.

Antiques
Main Street antique shops

Western antiques, handmade furniture, fine jewelry, sportswear. Mining-era buildings now hold the boutiques.

Books
Dolly's Bookstore

Independent bookstore on Main Street since 1971 — well-curated stacks, resident cat.

French — Dining
Adolph's

Continental fare with Swiss and French roots since 1976 — a Park City institution. White tablecloth, mountain view.

Market — Sunday
Park Silly Sunday Market

Sundays only — Main Street goes pedestrian. Local artisans, farmers, live music.

Outdoor — Quiet
McPolin Farm

The white barn on the way into town — historic 1922 farmstead, walking trails, photography.
