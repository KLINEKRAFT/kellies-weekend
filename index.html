Chapter 01

Bishop's
Lodge

An Auberge property tucked into 317 acres of high desert just outside Santa Fe. Originally the retreat of New Mexico's first archbishop. Reopened in 2021 after a top-down renovation. Adobe walls, kiva fireplaces, dark wood beams, a wellness program built around the land.

Rooms100Casitas, suites, and standalone homes
Acreage317Private acres, equestrian stables, trails
Elevation7,300ftHigh desert; cool 70s at night in July
From SF Plaza15minA short drive into town when you want it
SHEA — The Spa

A bathhouse, not a treatment menu.

SHEA reframes the spa as a sequence of rooms — eucalyptus steam, infrared sauna, cold plunge, soaking tubs — meant to be moved through unhurried over an afternoon. Treatments draw on Indigenous and Hispanic land-based traditions specific to northern New Mexico.

Day passes are included; private treatments book separately.

  • RenewalFull-body massage with high-desert botanical oils — sage, juniper, piñon. 80 minutes.
  • CuarzoCrystal-stone therapy unique to the property; rose quartz and clear quartz placed along the spine.
  • Sound BathGroup session in the meditation pavilion. Crystal bowls, gongs, an hour of nothing else to do.
  • BathhouseHalf-day access to the steam, sauna, plunge, and outdoor soaking pools.
  • EquineA grooming and groundwork session with the stables horses — not a ride, a slow hour of just being with them.

Chapter 02

Ojo
Santa Fe

A 70-acre spring-fed wellness resort 15 minutes from the Plaza. Sister property to Ojo Caliente. The whole place is built around the water — soaking pools, gardens, adobe casitas, no televisions in the rooms. The shortest-distance-to-stillness of the three.

Rooms53Casita suites, no TVs by design
Acreage70Spring-fed lakes, orchards, organic gardens
Soaking Pools8Mineral pools, kiva sauna, cold plunge
Phone PolicynoPhones discouraged in pool areas
The Spring & The Spa

Water is the program.

Ojo's whole logic is built around its private spring. Mineral-rich water is piped into eight soaking pools at different temperatures, each set among the cottonwoods. Treatments at the spa lean Ayurvedic — abhyanga, milagro stone massage, mud wraps drawn from on-site clay.

Day-of-arrival pool access is included with any room.

  • MilagroHot-stone treatment using black river stones gathered nearby; signature to the brand.
  • Mud WrapOn-site clay paired with a private outdoor soak. Best booked back-to-back.
  • AbhyangaWarm-oil Ayurvedic massage performed by two therapists in synchrony; 90 minutes.
  • Kiva SaunaAn adobe sauna built in the traditional pueblo round form. Open all day, no booking.
  • GardensWalk the organic farm and labyrinth before sunrise; coffee is brought out at 6.

Chapter 03

Wynn
Las Vegas

The Wynn is the Forbes five-star outlier on the Strip — the only North American resort to hold five-star ratings for both its hotel and spa for fifteen straight years. A different texture of weekend: a controlled, polished indoor world, walkable to everything, with the food and the bed quality that very few places can match.

Rooms4,748Across Wynn & Encore towers
Forbes Stars5+5Hotel and spa; only such property in N.A.
Spa SqFt45kTwo separate facilities — Wynn & Encore
Restaurants22Including five Michelin starred
The Spa at Wynn

The most decorated spa in the country.

Two separate spa facilities under one roof, each over 20,000 sq ft. Forbes five-star for fifteen consecutive years. The wet circuit — steam, sauna, rainfall showers, mineral pools — is open to all spa-day guests for the full day.

The food in this hotel is also genuinely the point. Five Michelin stars across the property.

  • Good LifeTheir signature: 100 minutes of brushing, polishing, full-body massage, and a scalp ritual.
  • HammamTraditional Moroccan hammam ritual — black soap, savon noir, kessa exfoliation. 90 minutes.
  • Wet LoungeFull-day access to the steam, sauna, vitality pool, and rainfall showers. Often the whole booking.
  • MizumiForbes five-star Japanese with a 90-ft waterfall outside the window. Order omakase.
  • DelilahA 1950s supper club inside the resort. Live music, prime rib, a wedding-cake-tier cocktail menu.

Las Vegas — eight hand-picked stops

Beyond the Strip
01
Antiquing
The 18b Arts District — Antiques Row
Three blocks of vintage and antique dealers in downtown's arts district — Patina Décor, The Glass Pony, Retro Vegas. Mid-century, Hollywood Regency, old casino ephemera. Better than anything on the Strip.
Main St & Charleston · 12 min via cab
02
Library — Special Collections
UNLV Special Collections — Showgirl Archive
Open to the public by appointment. The Lied Library's Special Collections holds the most complete archive of Las Vegas casino, showgirl, and Rat Pack ephemera in existence. Costume sketches, programs, signed press shots.
UNLV campus · 14 min
03
Mid-century
The Neon Museum — Night Tour
Outdoor "boneyard" of 250 restored mid-century signs from the original Strip. Book the after-dark Brilliant! Tour — many signs are re-illuminated with digital projection mapping. Visually unmatched.
770 Las Vegas Blvd N · 15 min
04
French
Bouchon — Thomas Keller
Inside the Venetian. Keller's French bistro — the steak frites, the roast chicken, the bread is theirs. Outdoor courtyard with a fountain when the evening cools. Lunch is the better booking.
Venetian Resort · 7 min walk
05
Art Museum
Bellagio Gallery of Fine Art
The Strip's serious small museum. Rotating shows from the world's major collections — recent runs have included Picasso, Yayoi Kusama, and the Whitney. One quiet room, one hour, then back into the noise.
Bellagio · 10 min walk
06
Bookstore
The Writer's Block
Independent bookstore in downtown's Fremont East, the city's best literary anchor. Curated fiction, a deep poetry section, and an indoor "artificial forest" installation that has to be seen. Coffee bar in the back.
519 S 6th St · 13 min
07
Day Trip
Valley of Fire — Sunrise Drive
45 minutes north of the Strip. Red sandstone, petroglyphs, no crowds before 9 a.m. in summer. Go at sunrise — back at the spa by 11. The single best morning you can have from a Vegas hotel.
State Route 169 · 50 min drive
08
Dinner
Bardot Brasserie — Michael Mina
Inside the Aria. A grown-up Parisian brasserie — escargot, sole meunière, a duck à l'orange that still rotates on a cart. Late-night French onion soup at the bar after a show.
Aria Resort · 9 min walk
